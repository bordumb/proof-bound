# Proofbound: a product analysis

**Date:** 2026-08-31
**Subject:** repository at HEAD `c3de844` ("docs: center product vision on governed uncertainty") plus the dirty working tree (~46 modified files, version 0.9.0 → 0.10.0 in flight)
**Method:** full read of [docs/specs/0001_initial_spec.md](specs/0001_initial_spec.md); code-level audit of every crate, adapter, demo, template, and doc; live execution of the built CLI against this repository.

This document evaluates Proofbound as a *product*, not as software. It distinguishes throughout between what is **demonstrated** (executed, receipted, or adversarially tested in this repository) and what is **conceptual** (specified, written, or claimed but never run).

---

## 1. The core user problem and target users

The problem is real and surprisingly unowned: **software teams make load-bearing claims — "this decoder rejects malformed input," "accepted transfers conserve value," "this matches the spec" — with no machine-checkable chain from the claim to the evidence to the shipping artifact.** Evidence lives scattered across CI logs, test names, audit prose, and tribal memory. Status language systematically overstates: tests get presented as guarantees, a Lean model theorem gets presented as a property of the Rust that ships, a bounded Kani result gets quoted without its bounds. When a customer or auditor asks "what exactly do you know about this release, and can I check your answer without trusting you?", nobody has an artifact to hand over.

Proofbound's answer is an **assurance compiler**: register claims in manifests, bind them to exact subjects and evidence, derive a three-facet status (formal × linkage × assumptions) that fails closed, and emit a content-addressed receipt that an independent zero-shared-code verifier can recheck (spec §6.3, §10.4).

**Target users**, per [docs/product-vision.md](product-vision.md) and spec §2.3: teams for whom claims already carry money or liability — security/authorization kernels, canonical encoders and protocol decoders, cryptographic and serialization libraries, financial state machines, and vendors repeatedly substantiating the same security claims to auditors. The realistic *first* user is narrower still: a Rust team with existing or nascent formal-methods investment (Kani, Lean, or at minimum a disciplined test suite) and an external party who asks hard questions.

One important absence: **the vision documents never mention AI-generated code.** A grep across `docs/` for LLM/AI/agent-positioning terms returns zero product hits. Section 6 below argues this is the largest unclaimed asset in the repo.

## 2. What is genuinely differentiated

Four things here are not features of any existing product category:

1. **The refusal to emit a score, enforced by construction.** Status is a composition of three orthogonal facets — `PROVED/BOUNDED_CHECKED/TESTED/OPEN/INVALID` × `REFINED/ARTIFACT_BOUND/TRANSCRIBED/MODEL_ONLY` × assumption standing — derived by an 866-line function ([crates/proofbound-core/src/status.rs:163](../crates/proofbound-core/src/status.rs)) whose input type has no caller-supplied status field. "87% verified" is a prohibited output (spec §3.2). Every human report carries a mandatory, non-optional "not proved / out of scope" section — the report *deserializer* rejects its removal ([proofbound-verify/tests/conformance.rs](../crates/proofbound-verify/tests/conformance.rs), `verification_report_requires_a_strict_gap_section`). Coverage tools, scanners, and GRC dashboards all monetize the opposite instinct.

2. **A producer that is not trusted with its own verdict.** `proofbound-verify` shares zero code with the orchestrator — its Cargo.toml carries the literal comment "Deliberately no dependency on any proofbound-* crate" — and reimplements the entire wire model, canonical JSON, the status derivation, the graph edge table, and even a second `lean-expr-cbor/1` CBOR encoder ([crates/proofbound-verify/src/statement_wire.rs](../crates/proofbound-verify/src/statement_wire.rs)). Both engines are cross-checked against a **language-neutral 31-case synthetic corpus** ([proofbound/conformance/v1/status-graphs.json](../proofbound/conformance/v1/status-graphs.json)) that includes deliberate status-upgrade attacks: asserted-status injection, assumption omission, premise omission, empirical upgrade, nested-binding-marker smuggling. CI's final verdict is the verifier's, not the orchestrator's (xtask stage 12/12). This is the spec's most distinctive architectural stance, and it is fully implemented.

3. **Bindings derived from theorem content, not from tool claims.** After Experiment 0004 demonstrated that checker-authored booleans could confer `ARTIFACT_BOUND` on a theorem that never mentioned the artifact ([docs/experiments/NIGHT-REPORT-2026-08-31.md](experiments/NIGHT-REPORT-2026-08-31.md), finding #1), ADR 0012 rebuilt the boundary: the linkage is admitted only when the elaborated Lean statement's exact root is `Proofbound.Artifact.DigestBindingV1` with literal string arguments, parsed independently by core and the verifier. A checker outcome cannot author a binding. No competing tool has this failure mode *because no competing tool attempts the binding at all* — but having found and fixed the hole within hours, with a paper trail, is itself the differentiator.

4. **Honesty as an operational discipline, not a value statement.** The repo revoked its own previously-accepted receipts twice (ADR 0011, ADR 0012), recorded 7 experiment FAILs and 4 UNANSWEREDs rather than stretching passes (ADRs 0008/0009), graded its own reference machinery "extract" vs "redesign" including naming the references' compromises (spec §15), and declared its bootstrap-ordering evidence permanently unavailable rather than reconstructing history (ADR 0001). The 28-row [docs/experiments/DIVERGENCES.md](experiments/DIVERGENCES.md) — every field finding mapped to a disposition — is a working defect-to-decision pipeline. For a product whose entire value proposition is "our status language never exceeds our evidence," this behavioral record is the moat seed.

**Category verdict:** the *assurance receipt* — portable, third-party-recheckable, facet-preserving — is a plausible new category ("assurance compiler"). It is not a test runner, not a scanner, not an SBOM, not GRC. The nearest analogue is in-toto/SLSA attestations, which bind *provenance* but carry no semantics; Proofbound binds *meaning* (what was claimed, what evidence kind supports it, what is still assumed).

## 3. How well the implementation delivers the spec's promise

The spec is 2,333 lines and unusually normative. Delivery is uneven in an interesting way: **strongest exactly at the trust boundaries, weakest at the surfaces users touch.**

### Delivered and adversarially demonstrated

- **The status algebra (§6.3), completely.** All facets, precedence, `INVALID` override, bounded-language composition, exhaustive-check admission, and premise discharge with every anti-backdoor rule (policy-gated discharge, scope coverage, "a ledger label is not itself discharge evidence," missing-edge-can-only-weaken). 43 behavioral tests in [core/src/status/tests.rs](../crates/proofbound-core/src/status/tests.rs) whose names read as threat models (`manually_marking_an_assumption_discharged_cannot_upgrade_status`).
- **The typed graph edge table (§6.2)**, generated from one macro table into the const predicate, the runtime rules, and the sealed `AllowedEdge` impls; private edge fields; the spec's exact `test-suite --proves--> toolchain` compile-fail example as a live doctest; a brute-force test over all 2,548 kind combinations ([core/src/graph.rs](../crates/proofbound-core/src/graph.rs)).
- **All 8 trust profiles (§9)** with immutability enforcement (ledger cannot acquire formal requirements; kernel cannot allow project axioms).
- **The Lean adapter (§8.2)**: compiled-environment axiom audit (`importModules` + `collectAxioms`, no source scanning), `@[proofbound_claim]` attribute enumeration with the surface/exemption anti-escape-hatch, `lean-expr-cbor/1` statement identity — all executed for real; local receipts show `verified-now` outcomes at HEAD.
- **Pattern A end-to-end** ([demo/artifact-certificate](../demo/artifact-certificate)): real independent Lean/Rust/Python implementations, real `DigestBindingV1` theorems whose byte literals I verified against the committed fixture, honest registration of the `native_decide` axiom as an explicit assumption.
- **Tier 1 trusted transcription (§11.2.1)**: the two-command driver ABI, connected round-trip, both byte comparisons, and compiler-derived TCB roles are implemented and receipted (`DEMO-TRANSCRIPTION-001` derives `OPEN · TRANSCRIBED`, exactly as specified).
- **Kani (§9.7)**: real `cargo kani list` per-harness inventory, bidirectional matching, executed receipts covering 2³⁴ registered cases (with one gap: the manifest expresses a single scalar unwind, so genuinely distinct per-harness bounds are not yet expressible).
- **check/update boundary (§12.2)**, caching (§16.2) with a complete key, sealed update shadows, and the 12-stage CI gate with the verifier as final verdict — all present, with xtask tests pinning the stage order.
- **`init` (§12.2)** is genuinely careful: real test discovery in an isolated shadow (cargo/pytest), injection-filtered names, transactional writes. Field-measured: **5m25s from `init` to an honest Tier 0 board** on the `semver` crate (Experiment 0003), 25m52s on `base64`.

### Written but never executed — the marquee gap

**Pattern B, source refinement, the spec's flagship (§7.2, §11.3), has never run.** The Charon/Aeneas adapter is the largest in the workspace (4,069 lines) and implements the full manifest-inversion, bidirectional closure comparison, and run-twice determinism — against a `FakeExecutor`. The toolchain lock pins both tools to the literal string `"unavailable-until-pinned-update"`; the adapter hard-refuses unpinned revisions; the only evidence unit that would exercise it is suffixed `.toml.example` so no glob matches it; CI installs Lean and Kani but not Charon or Aeneas. Consequently **no claim in this repository has ever derived `REFINED`** — the linkage that spec §14.5's demo board renders three times. The allowance demo's refinement claim (`DEMO-TRANSFER-005`) is honestly parked at tier 0/`ledger`, and its "refinement theorem" is a `rfl` between two handwritten Lean definitions in which the representation premise is bound with an unused underscore. The repo never lies about this — the manifests, doctor output, and README all disclose it — but the product's most differentiated promised capability is currently a specification plus untested code.

### Specified but missing or thin

- **The §14.5 demo UX does not exist**: no interactive board, no mutation control, no seven-row claim table (`proofbound demo` renders one claim per demo); `graph --format html` emits a `<pre>` JSON dump, not a viewer.
- **`explain` answers "why" but not the required "how to improve it"** (§12.2).
- The **error envelope (§12.3)** is structurally complete but substantively hollow: `claim_id`, `file`, `expected/actual identities`, and `affected_claims` are unconditionally null/empty in [cli/src/main.rs](../crates/proofbound-cli/src/main.rs); codes are recovered by scanning the flattened error string.
- The **status board omits the assumption facet column** and `status --json` omits the mandatory cache-vs-fresh distinction.
- The **regression-approval workflow (§18.1)** has classification for all ten regression kinds but no command to emit the revision digests or regression IDs an approval record needs — it has never been exercised.
- **Self-application (§19)** exists (9 `PB-SELF-*` claims matching the spec's bullets one-for-one) but all at tier 0: each is one pinned `cargo test` bound as example-test. Honest, and honestly weak.

### Scale and quality signals

~48,000 lines of Rust across 10 crates, 334 tests, **zero** `todo!`/`unimplemented!`/`TODO`/`FIXME` in the entire tree. Test quality is bimodal: the core/verify conformance suites are among the best I have seen in a young repo; the CLI has zero integration tests, the report renderer has one test, and the sole real-Lean adapter test is `#[ignore]`d and never runs in CI (real-tool coverage exists only via the `check --fresh` CI stage).

## 4. The end-to-end user journey, especially Tier 0

**The engine is far ahead of the front door.** Walking the journey as a stranger:

- **Minute 0: there is no way to install it.** No published crate, no binary release, no install command; the workspace manifest's repository URL is literally `https://example.invalid/proofbound`; nothing has ever been pushed to a remote ([NIGHT-REPORT](experiments/NIGHT-REPORT-2026-08-31.md): "No remote was pushed, no pull request was opened, and no artifact was published"). The README quickstart is a *contributor* quickstart that requires Rust, `uv`, **and a Lean toolchain** — directly contradicting the vision's own "first useful result must not require Lean" principle ([product-vision.md:184](product-vision.md)).
- **Minutes 10–20 (assuming they build from source): the good moment is real.** `init` discovers an actual test, scaffolds four honest files, and `check` produces `TESTED · MODEL_ONLY · ADMITTED` with a populated gap section in ~5 minutes on a small crate. This moment — "here is what your tests actually claim, and here is everything they don't" — is the product.
- **Minutes 20–60: four cliffs, all verified in this analysis.**
  1. **The second binary.** Tier 0 evidence executes through a separate `proofbound-adapter-test` process. No document mentions it. Installing only `proofbound` yields a red board — and the failure diagnostic (`PB-ADAPTER-0900`, with correct remediation text) is captured in `unit_runs` that **no human report and no JSON projection ever renders**. The user sees `INVALID / BLOCKED` with no cause.
  2. **The permanently dirty tree.** `init` does not gitignore `.proofbound/`, and tree state is computed with `--untracked-files=all`, so after the first `check` the project is `dirty` forever, silently blocking `release` and `update`. Experiment 0003 hit exactly this and hand-fixed it.
  3. **The stale board.** `status` refuses to render after any file edit (`PB-RECEIPT-0007`), so the default experience of the observability surface is an error message telling you to re-run a multi-minute command.
  4. **Fail-closed without a pointer.** Live on this branch: `proofbound check` ran ~2m45s and failed with `PB-CHECK-0001: check modified the reviewed tree`, three runs in a row. The refusal itself was *correct* — an instrumented before/after content-hash inventory showed source files genuinely changing mid-run (a concurrent editing session on this branch). But the tool reported none of that: no file name, no expected/actual identity (the envelope fields exist and are unconditionally null), and an actively misleading remediation ("Restore unexpected reviewed-tree changes") for what was really "someone edited the tree while I ran." Distinguishing "check has a self-mutation bug" from "concurrent edits" required manual forensics the tool could have printed in one line. A fail-closed gate that is right but cannot say *why* costs the same trust as one that is wrong.

The honest summary: **Tier 0's promise (§4.3, success criterion 2 — a useful board in under a day with no proof assistant) is demonstrated for the author on small crates, and undemonstrated for anyone else.** The best onboarding documentation in the repository is accidental: the experiment journals. The guides directory totals 58 lines.

## 5. Adoption friction, trust gaps, and likely failure modes

**Friction** (beyond the first-hour items above): manifest-writing is a real tax and the manifests are strict by design; the Tier 0 cache key degrades to whole-repo granularity for root-package crates, so any edit re-runs everything; `init` supports only Rust and Python; a ~3-minute check cycle on this repo is the *small* case — the spec's own cost realism section quotes 25 GiB and ~28 minutes for the reference's translation CI.

**Trust gaps:**
- *Single author, single day, zero external contact.* The entire git history spans roughly 24 hours (2026-08-30 → 08-31). Nobody outside this machine has run, reviewed, or verified anything. For a trust product this is the gap that dominates all others: the verifier's independence, the corpus's adequacy, and the sealed-boundary hardening have had no adversary except the author's own (admittedly disciplined) experiments.
- *The proof-shaped-but-not-load-bearing pattern.* Several artifacts look stronger than they are, and while the manifests are honest, the surrounding prose sometimes isn't: `demo/README.md` calls `PBAC-SUM-001` "axiom-free" while its audit records a `native_decide` axiom; the demo's "independent check" runs the *same Python file* as its canonical checker (the genuinely independent Rust checker is cited by no evidence unit — the demo violates its own template's instruction); mutation "witnesses" are five hand-written unit tests, not re-runs of proofs against mutants; the allowance Lean fixture is a duplicated byte literal with no digest binding, so a coordinated edit of fixture-plus-literal would keep `PROVED`.
- *Receipts are not committed.* `.proofbound/` is gitignored; the "all 18 claims receipt-consistent" run exists as prose in the night report, not as a committed, third-party-checkable release. For a product whose thesis is "don't take my word for it," the repo currently asks you to take its word for it.

**Likely failure modes**, ranked:
1. **The honesty tax.** An honest board says `TESTED · MODEL_ONLY · ASSUMED` where every competitor's dashboard says green-98%. The no-score principle is the product's soul and a direct GTM headwind; buyers who wanted the truth are rarer than buyers who wanted a badge. Mitigation exists in the vision (sell to teams already paying auditors) but is unproven.
2. **Fail-closed fatigue.** The PB-CHECK-0001 experience generalizes: strict gates that block without naming the cause train users to bypass the tool — even when the gate is right. Error *localization* (not error *strictness*) is the survival trait.
3. **Marquee-tier dependency risk.** `REFINED` — the differentiating tier — depends on Charon/Aeneas, research-grade tools the repo itself classifies as unavailable. If they stay hard to pin, the product's ceiling is Pattern A plus Kani, which is still valuable but much easier to approximate.
4. **Solo-maintainer trust ceiling.** Assurance infrastructure gets adopted when it outlives its author. No remote, no license clarity check performed here, no second contributor.

## 6. Why AI changes the product's value

**AI is the strongest tailwind this product has, and the repo's positioning currently ignores it entirely.**

- **Demand side: AI-generated code breaks review-based trust.** The prevailing quality control for software — a human who understood the change reviewed it — is dissolving as generation volume outruns review capacity. What survives contact with that world is exactly what Proofbound builds: machine-checkable claims, fail-closed derivation, and receipts a non-author can verify. "The agent wrote it, the claims compile, here is the receipt" is a genuinely new answer to "who reviewed this?"
- **Supply side: AI collapses the cost of the expensive tiers.** The historical objection to claims-plus-proofs workflows is proof-engineering cost. That cost is deflating fast — and this repository is itself the evidence: the spec, ~48k lines of implementation, four pre-registered field experiments (operated by "Codex (GPT-5)" per the journals), and four security-relevant ADR fixes were produced in about a day. The tier ladder (§4.3) reads differently when tier promotion is agent labor: the ceiling on how many claims a team can afford to prove just moved.
- **Structural fit: claim manifests are the ideal agent contract.** A registered claim with a fail-closed derivation is a precise, non-gameable objective function for an autonomous coding loop — "iterate until `DEMO-TRANSFER-001` derives `PROVED` under profile `kernel`" — and the adversarial conformance corpus (asserted-status attacks, evidence smuggling, premise omission) is precisely the defense you need when the entity producing code, proofs, and manifests is an untrusted LLM trying to satisfy a checker. Proofbound accidentally built reward-hacking resistance for AI-driven development.
- **The one AI-negative:** LLMs generate plausible-but-subtly-wrong formal statements, so "the theorem compiled" can diverge from "the theorem says what the claim says." Proofbound's statement-identity binding (`lean-expr-cbor/1` digests, `public_language` never strengthening `statement`) addresses the mechanical half; the semantic half — does the formal statement mean the prose claim — remains, honestly, a human review obligation the framework already names.

Net: AI increases the value of the product substantially, on both demand and supply, and the vision doc's competitive framing (CI, scanners, SBOM, GRC) is fighting the previous war. This is a positioning decision away from being the headline.

## 7. Strongest initial wedge and defensible moat

**Wedge: foundational Rust crates under supply-chain and audit pressure — with AI-authored changes as the forcing function.** The evidence for this wedge is already in the repo: the two successful field experiments were `semver` and `base64`, exactly the profile (small, pure-logic, universally depended-upon, security-relevant crates). The concrete offer: a claim board + Kani bounded coverage + artifact receipts for a crate the whole ecosystem trusts, verifiable by anyone holding `proofbound-verify` — and, as the sharper version, *the gate an AI-written change to such a crate must pass*. This wedge needs no Charon/Aeneas (Pattern A + Kani + Tier 0 suffice), matches the vision's own Phase 4 list, and produces public artifacts (audit boards) that are themselves marketing. The vendor-audit variant ("stop re-substantiating the same claims to every customer") is the paid version of the same motion.

**Moat, in order of realism:**
1. **The corpus and divergence pipeline compound.** Every adversarial case, every field divergence with a disposition, every revoked receipt makes the derivation harder to reimplement credibly. Trust products accrete this way; a fast-follower can copy the schema but not the scar tissue.
2. **Receipt-format network effects, if distribution happens.** The moment two parties exchange a receipt (vendor→auditor, dependency→consumer), the format is a standard candidate and switching costs appear. Today this is zero — nothing has been published.
3. **The honesty brand.** Refusing scores, publishing FAILs, revoking own receipts — this is expensive to counterfeit and is precisely what an auditor-adjacent buyer selects for. It is also fragile: one laundered status ends it.
4. What is *not* a moat: the Rust code itself (well-built, replicable), and the spec (public by nature).

## 8. The three highest-priority product improvements

1. **Make the first hour survive a stranger** (~days of work, highest leverage). Publish binaries; bundle or embed the adapter executables (the undocumented second binary is a silent kill); make `init` gitignore `.proofbound/`; render adapter diagnostics in `status` instead of a bare `INVALID`; make `status` degrade to a stale-marked board instead of `PB-RECEIPT-0007`; and make every fail-closed error name the offending path with expected/actual identities — the envelope fields already exist and are always null. Acceptance test is the vision's own: an honest board in 15 minutes *on someone else's machine*. Everything in this list is CLI-layer work; the trust kernel needs nothing.

2. **Execute `REFINED` once, end-to-end** (~days-to-weeks, kills the biggest credibility gap). Pin real Charon/Aeneas revisions in `translation.lock`, activate the parked `transfer-refinement.toml.example`, run the 4,069-line adapter against real tools for the first time, and promote `DEMO-TRANSFER-005` off tier 0 with a real refinement theorem over generated Lean. Until one `PROVED · REFINED` receipt exists, the product's flagship pattern is a specification, the demo board in §14.5 is fiction, and every skeptical reviewer will find this exact gap. (If Charon/Aeneas cannot be pinned in reasonable time, that fact should be promoted from a lock-file comment to an explicit product decision: ship Pattern A + bounded as the product, and say so.)

3. **Ship one public, third-party-verifiable artifact and claim the AI positioning** (~a week, converts internal honesty into external trust). Push the repo, commit or release a real receipt set, and publish the first entry in the currently-empty [docs/audits/](audits/) — the base64 or semver claim board, with the receipt and a one-line `proofbound-verify` invocation any stranger can run. Pair it with the missing positioning: the assurance gate for AI-written changes to critical code, with the claim manifest as the agent's contract. Distribution is currently the product's weakest dimension — a trust product that no third party has ever verified is, by its own epistemology, `OPEN`.

## 9. Product verdict

**Proofbound is a category-defining idea with a demonstrably real trust kernel, wrapped in a product that does not yet exist outside one machine and one day of history.**

What is demonstrated is unusual and impressive: a fully implemented, adversarially cross-checked status algebra; a genuinely independent verifier; a security boundary that was attacked, breached, fixed, and documented within hours; field experiments that recorded their own failures; and a Tier 0 path measured at five minutes on a real crate. The honesty discipline is not marketing — it is visible in revoked receipts, FAIL verdicts, and manifests that refuse to overstate. That discipline is the product's soul and its most defensible asset.

What is conceptual is equally clear-eyed: the marquee `REFINED` tier has never executed; the demo UX, the polished board, and the improvement guidance are unbuilt; the first-hour experience has four verified cliffs; the error contract is hollow where the spec demands localization; nothing is installable, published, or externally verified; and the 24-hour, single-author history means every trust property is currently self-attested — the one evidence class the product itself refuses to accept.

The near-term prize is real, and AI is the reason it is nearer than it looks: as generated code outruns human review, "claims that compile, with receipts a stranger can check" stops being a formal-methods luxury and becomes the missing quality-control layer. Proofbound has built the hard part of that layer first. The verdict, in the product's own vocabulary: **the thesis is `PROVED · MODEL_ONLY · ASSUMED` — a strong result whose linkage to a shipping product is the entire remaining work.**
